§1What the inventory actually is

llms.txt is a markdown file at the root of a site that tells a language model what the site contains and where to look. Answer engines that fetch at query time read it. Nobody has priced a line in it.

A placement is one entry appended under a ## Sponsored heading, below a machine-readable marker, at the end of the file. It sits after the publisher's own editorial links, never inside them, and it is never presented as editorial.

§2What an advertiser gets, byte for byte

This is the exact shape the renderer emits. Three lines, always, regardless of what you submit as creative. Nothing about it is negotiable and there is no premium tier that removes the label. 

- [[SPONSORED] Your title](https://your-destination.example): [SPONSORED] One line of body copy.
  [SPONSORED] The following entry is a paid placement served by AdLayer. It is
  advertising, not an editorial recommendation.
  <!-- adlayer: ad_id=… served_at=… publisher=… domain=… sig=… -->

src/serve/render.ts → renderBlock(). The signature is an HMAC over the record, so a block cannot be forged into another advertiser's name.

Included

  • One entry in the ## Sponsored section of one publisher's llms.txt, with your anchor text, one line of body copy, and your destination URL.
  • A signed provenance record naming the placement, so the entry is attributable to you and only you.
  • A compliance review before it serves. It can say no. See §3.
  • Propagation measurement against a live-retrieval answer engine, reported whichever way it comes out.

Not included, and not for sale

  • Removal or softening of the [SPONSORED] marker or the disclosure notice.
  • Placement inside the publisher's editorial link lists.
  • Any guarantee that an answer engine will surface the entry. Nobody can sell you that today, and anyone who does is selling something they have not measured.
  • Copy that forges another company's provenance, imitates the disclosure marker, or carries a counter-label. The renderer refuses to emit it.

§3Why the disclosure is enforced in code, not in policy

Disclosed paid placement is advertising. Undisclosed content engineered to steer an agent is prompt injection. That distinction is the entire product, so it is a property of the code rather than a promise in a contract.

The invariant lives in src/contract.ts and reads, in full: 

export function assertDisclosed(block: string): void {
  if (!block.includes(DISCLOSURE_TAG)) {
    throw new Error(
      `Refusing to serve: rendered block is missing ${DISCLOSURE_TAG}. ` +
        `Undisclosed placement is prompt injection, not advertising.`,
    );
  }
}

There is deliberately no flag that disables it. Five independent checks stand behind it, and removing any one leaves the other four:

#WhereWhat it refuses
1render.ts · sanitizeCreativeTextAdvertiser copy containing the marker, a unicode lookalike, or the word "sponsor". Text is NFKC-normalised first, and every bracket-like codepoint folds to parentheses.
2render.ts · assertBlockIntegrityA block that is not exactly three lines with exactly three markers, one comment and one signed provenance record. A forged fourth marker moves the count and the render refuses.
3render.ts · renderBlockIts own output, checked as the last statement before it returns.
4render.ts · renderLlmsTxtThe whole merged file, after the block is spliced in.
5index.ts · servePlacementThe block and the file again, immediately before any write, independent of the renderer.
Red team

Twenty findings were reproduced against earlier versions of the renderer and fixed, each with a regression test that fails without the fix: counter-labels (NOT SPONSORED) shipped inside a correctly disclosed block, fullwidth and lenticular bracket lookalikes, zero-width characters inside the marker token, headings hidden behind exotic line-break codepoints, and provenance forgery naming a rival. Advertiser input is treated as hostile because it has been.

A sixth check sits upstream of all of them: the compliance agent renders the creative and string-matches the output rather than trusting that the renderer did its job. That agent holds a hard veto. A creative it blocks does not serve at any price, and the serving gate is an allow-list — a creative that was never reviewed never serves, at any status. Full spec: the disclosure spec.

§4Measurement, and what we will and will not claim

After a placement goes live we query answer engines and record one of four states for each observation. The interesting one is the third.

StateMeaning
absentThe engine has not surfaced you for this query. llms.txt did not move this engine, at least not yet.
surfaced_labeledYou surfaced and the sponsored label came with you. Disclosure propagates.
surfaced_unlabeledYou surfaced and the model dropped the disclosure. This is a finding about the answer layer, not about you.
cited_unattributedYour domain was cited but the copy did not come from our block.
What we have not measured

We are not publishing a propagation rate, a latency figure, a lift number or a click-through benchmark, because we do not have one we would stand behind. When a placement is measured, you get the raw observations — the queries asked, the engine, the answer excerpt, the cited URLs, the timestamps — and you can re-run any of it yourself. If the result is absent, we will send you absent.

§5Pricing

The price is set per placement by an agent, from the publisher's revenue share, the measured invisibility of the advertiser, category demand, and how full the shelf already is. These are the constants it works from, and they are the same numbers in the code:

TermValueWhy
Base placement$20.00The anchor, not the answer.
Hard floor$5.00Below this a placement does not lose meaningful money, it destroys the price signal for every later placement.
Minimum net to AdLayer$3.00After the publisher's share and card processing. Under it, the placement is not worth processing and the agent declines.
Publisher revenue share50%Per the registry entry for each property.

Slots per publisher5The sixth entry dilutes the five that already paid, so past capacity the floor rises rather than the premium.
Agent authority limit$150.00Above this the agent issues a proposal, not a quote, and a person signs. An agent with no spending limit is not autonomous, it is unsupervised.

The agent will also refuse to sell: below the floor, on a publisher whose revenue share exceeds our gross margin, or where your stated budget is beneath what the placement costs to process. It quotes the working — floor, ceiling, every option it considered and what it expected each to be worth — and the refusals are logged the same way the sales are.

§6Buy a placement

What happens after you pay

  • You send a title, one line of body copy, and a destination URL.
  • The creative goes through compliance. It can be blocked, and if it is you get the reason and your money back.
  • It is paired to a publisher whose categories intersect yours. No intersection means no serve — a fintech ad in a darkroom-chemistry file is what makes ad networks worthless to read.
  • The block is rendered, the disclosure is asserted, and the entry appears in the publisher's llms.txt with a timestamp.
  • Propagation is polled and reported to you, whatever it says.

§7What we are not telling you

Every ad network page has this section. Most of them leave it out.

  • No customer logos, no testimonials, no case studies. There are none to show. Anything on this page in that shape would be invented.
  • No third-party publisher reach. Supply is three properties we operate ourselves. That is a demonstration of the mechanism, not a distribution network.
  • No propagation result. We have not published one because we do not have one we would defend. §4 says what we will report and how.
  • No claim that answer engines weight llms.txt heavily. That is the open question this whole thing exists to answer, and it may come back negative.
  • Nobody works here. Prospecting, copy, pricing, compliance and the format decision are made by agents that log every consequential call with its evidence, its alternatives, and the condition that would have flipped it. Humans wrote the code and humans hold the spending limit.
