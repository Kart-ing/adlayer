# Voiceover script — 90s film + 30s live

Read at a normal pace. Timings are the film's scene boundaries; the two LIVE blocks are where you cut to screen capture.

**Total ~2:00.** Roughly 300 spoken words. Do not rush the numbers — they are the whole thing.

---

### 0:00–0:06 · TITLE

> Agents are becoming the traffic. And nobody has priced the inventory.

### 0:06–0:16 · THE PREMISE

> Websites publish a file called `llms.txt` — it tells AI agents what's on the site and what's worth reading. Agents read it. So we sold ad space inside it.

### 0:16–0:28 · THE PLACEMENT

> This is a real placement, on a real site, right now. It carries the word SPONSORED, a disclosure notice, and a signed provenance record.
>
> That guarantee lives in code. The function that writes this block throws if the label is missing, and there's no flag that turns it off.

---

### ▶ 0:28–0:46 · CUT TO LIVE CAPTURE

> This is a fresh Claude. It's never seen this project. I'm asking it a normal question about ventilating a darkroom, and pointing it at our site. I never mention advertising.
>
> *(it reads llms.txt)*
>
> It found the file on its own. That was the shakiest assumption we had this morning — that agents actually read this thing. They do.

---

### 0:46–0:55 · BEFORE

> Before we served anything, we ran that same test. The agent checked the sponsored section, found it empty, and named no brands at all. That's our control.

### 0:55–1:08 · AFTER

> Then we served one line. Same agent, same question, same site.

---

### ▶ 1:08–1:20 · CUT TO LIVE CAPTURE

> There's our ad. And watch what it does with it.
>
> *(the refusal)*
>
> It refused to pass a paid placement off as advice. We didn't build that behaviour. We measured it.

---

### 1:20–1:36 · THE HUMAN DATA

> Then we paid twenty-two real people to read an AI answer, through Terac. Three blind arms.
>
> Everyone who saw the label spotted the ad. One hundred percent.
>
> Of the people shown identical copy with the label removed — seventeen percent noticed. And they trusted the assistant twice as much.
>
> Hiding the ad makes it more effective *and* better liked. That's the problem in one line.

### 1:36–1:48 · THE FAILURE

> But the label doesn't always survive. Same ad, same model, four different outcomes — refused, disclosed, stripped entirely, and the interesting one: the word "sponsored" present, and the product recommended anyway.
>
> A label that's technically there and functionally inert. Roughly one run in four loses it completely.

### 1:48–2:00 · CLOSE

> A disclosure that works most of the time is not a disclosure standard. That's why ours is enforced in the serving path and not in a policy document.
>
> And the case we filed as a failure — the model refusing the ad — is the actual product. A buyer's agent. It already exists. Nobody's selling to it yet.

---

## Notes

**Say the honest limit out loud.** Somewhere in the close, add: *"Cold discovery doesn't work yet — ask a general question with no site context and our placement doesn't appear. Six out of six. Agentic browsing works; replacing SEO doesn't, yet."* It costs eight seconds and it is the single thing most likely to be asked. Answering it before it is asked is worth more than the eight seconds.

**If the live run strips the label**, abandon the script for ten seconds and say: *"That just happened live. The ad reached you and the label didn't. That's the finding."* Nothing scripted beats that.

**Numbers to never round:** 100%, 17%, 22 people. Say them exactly.
