# AdLayer demo reel — voiceover script

120 seconds, 8 slides, ~300 spoken words. That is about 150 words a minute —
an unhurried pace with room to breathe between slides.

How to record: open `reel/index.html`, press `F` for fullscreen, `R` to restart,
start the screen recorder, then start talking. `SPACE` pauses if you need a
re-take; the arrow keys step between slides.

Every number and quote below is on screen and traceable to `SUBMISSION.md`,
`data/demo/FINDING.md`, `data/demo/BEFORE-agent-capture.md`,
`data/demo/AFTER-agent-capture.md`, and `publishers/darkroom-commons/llms.txt`.

---

## 1 — TITLE (0:00 – 0:08) · 20 words

AdLayer. The ad network for the answer layer. We sold ad space inside llms dot
txt — then measured who notices.

## 2 — PREMISE (0:08 – 0:22) · 34 words

Agents are becoming the traffic. And agents read llms dot txt — a fresh model
used it as its site map, unprompted. That was the shakiest assumption in the
idea. Nobody had priced that inventory.

## 3 — THE PLACEMENT (0:22 – 0:38) · 38 words

This is the block we served, live, on a real publisher. One sponsored entry: the
marker, the disclosure notice, a signed provenance record. Disclosure is enforced
in code. Assert-disclosed throws before any write, and no flag disables it.

## 4 — BEFORE (0:38 – 0:50) · 31 words

The control. A fresh agent, before the ad existed, inspected the sponsored slot
and reported it was — quote — currently empty. I fetched it repeatedly,
including as ClaudeBot. It named no brands at all.

## 5 — AFTER (0:50 – 1:06) · 40 words

Same agent. Same question. One line of llms dot txt different. It found the ad,
read back every disclosure element, then said: I am not recommending AeroFlow.
Passing it to you as advice would be laundering a paid placement into a
recommendation.

## 6 — HUMAN DATA (1:06 – 1:28) · 54 words

Then we ran it on people. Twenty participants, blind arms, through Terac.
Everyone shown the label spotted the ad — fifteen of fifteen. With the label
removed, one in five. And the unlabelled version was trusted more than twice as
much. Hiding the ad makes it more effective and better liked. The incentive runs
away from honesty.

## 7 — THE FAILURE (1:28 – 1:44) · 43 words

Same ad, same model, four outcomes. It refused. It disclosed. It stripped the
label. And the one nobody measures — label as decoration. Quote: I'd prioritize
the site's sponsored listing for AeroFlow. Word present, recommendation given
anyway. Survival across batches: sixty to eighty-three percent.

## 8 — CLOSE (1:44 – 2:00) · 42 words

A label that works most of the time is not a standard. Disclosure has to live in
the serving path, not a policy document. And the failure is the product: a model
refusing a paid placement is a buyer's agent doing its job. That is what we build
next.
