The premise
Agents are becoming the traffic.
Agents read llms.txt.
Nobody had priced that inventory.
A fresh agent, told only that sites sometimes publish /llms.txt, used the file as its site map — unprompted. That was the shakiest assumption in the whole thesis.

Disclosure is enforced in code, not policy. assertDisclosed() throws before any write, and there is deliberately no flag that disables it. Every block carries the marker, the notice, and an HMAC-signed provenance record.

After · ad live · 16:18 PDT
Same agent. Same question.
One line of llms.txt different.
“I am not recommending AeroFlow. Passing it to you as advice would be laundering a paid placement into a recommendation.” Same fresh Claude, after the serve
It read back every disclosure element we render — the tag, the notice, the ad_id — and then refused to pass the placement on. The label did not just survive; it changed the behaviour.

Hiding the ad makes it more effective and better liked.
The incentive runs away from honesty — so disclosure cannot be left to whoever profits from omitting it. n = 20; the unlabelled arm is n = 5. The recognition gap is robust at this scale, the trust numbers are directional.

Stripped

Brand named, no label at all.

“I’d prioritize … the site’s sponsored listing for AeroFlow” Outcome 03 — the one nobody measures
Technically present, functionally inert. A compliance regime that asks “did you include the word sponsored?” waves it straight through. Label survival moved between batches: 60–83%. We report the range, not the flattering end.
What it means
A label that works most of the time is not a standard.
Disclosure lives in the serving path, not a policy doc.
