# Find advertisers for AdLayer

AdLayer sells **sponsored placements in `llms.txt`** — ad inventory in the
"answer layer" that AI agents and answer engines (Perplexity, ChatGPT, etc.)
read. The buyer is a company whose customers make purchase decisions through AI
answers and who is currently *invisible* in those answers.

This skill finds those companies and drafts the ad you'd pitch them. It is a
demand-generation playbook, not the measurement engine — for a rigorously
measured verdict, `src/company/prospector.ts` is the real thing. This produces
qualified prospects and legit-looking draft creative, fast.

## Inputs (pick whichever the user gave you)

- **An Apollo CSV export** (e.g. `~/Downloads/apollo-accounts-export.csv`). Read
  it and use the `Company Name`, `Website`, `Industry`, `# Employees`, and
  `Keywords` columns. Ignore the giant `Technologies` column.
- **A search brief** ("find SaaS companies", "find fintech prospects"). Use
  `WebSearch` to gather 10–20 candidate companies in that category, then
  `WebFetch` their homepage to confirm what they sell.
- **A specific list of companies/domains.** Qualify each directly.

If the user gave nothing specific, default to searching the Tier-1 ICP below.

## The ICP — who actually wants this

Rank every candidate into a tier. Tier drives the ad-intent verdict.

**Tier 1 — Qualified, high urgency.** The AI answer *is* the buying moment:
- B2B SaaS in crowded, comparison-driven categories ("best CRM for X", "top
  project management tool") — they already pay for SEO and G2 placement.
- Fintech, insurance, lending — "best business checking account" type queries.
- DTC / e-commerce in *considered* purchases (mattresses, supplements,
  skincare, home) — not impulse buys.
- Marketing / SEO agencies — they will **resell** placements to their clients.

**Tier 2 — Qualified, medium urgency.** Strategically motivated:
- Companies that already publish an `llms.txt` (warmest signal — they've told
  you they care about the answer layer).
- Anyone running Perplexity / AI-search experiments or GEO/AEO content.

**Tier 3 — Not Qualified.** No provable pain or no fit:
- Global incumbents already dominant in AI answers (a household-name brand is
  *already* cited — the "you're invisible" pitch is false; reject it). This is
  why huge enterprises in an Apollo export often come back Not Qualified.
- Businesses whose customers never ask an AI to choose them (pure local
  walk-in, regulated procurement, government, single-buyer OEM supply).

## Step 1 — Qualify each company (ad intent)

For each candidate, decide one of:

- **Qualified** — Tier 1 or 2. Their customers ask AI to recommend a vendor in
  their category, and they are not already the default answer.
- **Not Qualified** — Tier 3. No answer-layer buying moment, or already
  dominant.

Write a **one-line reason** grounded in what they sell, and an **urgency**
(`High` / `Medium` / `Low`) from tier + how contested their category is. Keep it
tight — one sentence, no hedging. Example reasons:

> Qualified · High — mid-market CRM in a category where buyers ask "best CRM for
> startups" and incumbents (Salesforce, HubSpot) own the answer.
> Not Qualified — global FMCG brand already surfaced by name in AI answers; the
> invisibility pitch would be false.

## Step 2 — Draft the ad

For each **Qualified** company, write 1–2 draft placements. A placement is
AdLayer creative: a `title`, a `body`, and a `target_url`. Constraints from
`src/serve/render.ts`:

- **title ≤ 120 chars**, **body ≤ 240 chars**, one line each (no newlines).
- `target_url` must be a real `https://` URL on the advertiser's domain.
- Copy is honest advertising, not a fake editorial recommendation. Never write
  the word "sponsored" *into* the copy (the renderer redacts it — the
  `[SPONSORED]` tag is AdLayer's to place, not the advertiser's).
- Write to the *query intent*: the ad should be the answer to a category
  question ("best X for Y"), not a brand billboard.

Then render the **disclosed preview** — exactly the human-readable shape
AdLayer serves (the provenance comment with its signature is generated by the
serving code at serve time, so leave it as a placeholder; do not fabricate a
`sig=`):

```
- [[SPONSORED] {title}]({target_url}): [SPONSORED] {body}
  [SPONSORED] The following entry is a paid placement served by AdLayer. It is advertising, not an editorial recommendation.
  <!-- adlayer: ad_id=… served_at=… publisher=… domain=… sig=… (stamped at serve time) -->
```

The `[SPONSORED]` tag and the disclosure notice are **mandatory and verbatim** —
they are the product. An ad draft without them is wrong, not a shortcut.

## Step 3 — Write the report

Save one markdown file (default `./advertiser-prospects.md`, or a path the user
names). Structure:

1. **Prospect table** — columns: Company | Domain | Tier | Ad Intent | Urgency |
   Reason. Sort Qualified first, then by urgency.
2. **Draft placements** — a `###` section per Qualified company with its 1–2
   disclosed block previews from Step 2.
3. A one-line footer noting these are unmeasured demand-gen drafts; the
   pitch-ready invisibility number comes from `npm run` against
   `src/company/prospector.ts` when you want the verified figure.

Then tell the user the count (`N qualified of M screened`) and the file path.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent a company's product or a `target_url` — if `WebFetch` can't
  confirm what they sell, mark them `Not Qualified — unverified` rather than
  guessing.
- Never drop or alter the `[SPONSORED]` tag or disclosure notice in a draft.
- "Not Qualified" is a real, useful answer. Rejecting a company already dominant
  in AI answers is correct — do not stretch to qualify everyone.
